Most websites don't lose customers because they look bad — they lose customers because of small, fixable friction points that add up. Here are five of the most common ones.

## 1. Slow page load times

Every extra second of load time increases the chance a visitor leaves before they even see your offer. Large unoptimized images and heavy, unused scripts are usually the biggest culprits.

## 2. A confusing navigation menu

If a visitor can't find what they're looking for in a few seconds, they'll assume it isn't there. Menus should be short, clearly labeled, and organized around what customers actually search for — not internal team structure.

## 3. No clear call to action

Every page should answer one question for the visitor: "What do I do next?" A page without a clear, visible call to action leaves that decision to chance.

## 4. A checkout or contact process with too many steps

Every additional field or click in a checkout or contact form is a chance for someone to give up. Cutting a form down to the essentials alone can meaningfully improve completion rates.

## 5. No social proof

Reviews, testimonials, and trust badges reduce the perceived risk of buying from you. A page with zero proof that real people have used your product or service asks visitors to take that leap of faith alone.
